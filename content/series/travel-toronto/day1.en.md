## VIA Rail Canada
Since this trip happened during spring break, one week before midterms, flights were not as cheap as I had hoped. So I decided to take the train to Toronto instead. The trip takes about five and a half hours. When I booked the outbound ticket, I noticed the price difference between economy and business class for the train I wanted was pretty small, so I went with business class. After using [Poly's 15% discount](https://www.polymtl.ca/durable/rabais-de-15-rail), the final price with tax was 179.16 CAD.

Overall, the business-class experience was genuinely great. As someone from Kaohsiung, I kept thinking about how a one-and-a-bit-hour high-speed rail trip between Kaohsiung and Taipei already costs around 1,500 TWD(~68CAD), and suddenly VIA business class didn't feel that expensive anymore.

Before boarding, business-class passengers can use the lounge, where there are free juices, drinks, and coffee machines. Business-class passengers also get priority boarding, and when it's time, they announce it in the lounge.

{{<img src="/images/toronto_trip/via_lounge.jpg" alt="VIA Rail Montreal Lounge">}}

The seats on the train were spacious, and each carriage had luggage space. There was also a place to hang your coat beside the seat, which was very appreciated because winter coats take up so much room.

During the five-and-a-half-hour trip, staff came by with drinks around five times. There was tea, coffee, red and white wine, and a few soft drinks.  
My train was roughly from 9 a.m. to 2 p.m., and the proper meal was served around noon. Before that, they gave out a small cake and a biscuit, and after the meal there was a piece of chocolate. Everything tasted good, and the portions were generous enough that I never worried about being hungry.

{{<img src="/images/toronto_trip/via_snacks.jpg" alt="VIA Rail Business Class snacks">}}

I had pre-ordered my meal through the VIA app before boarding and picked the salmon. When I opened the box and saw what looked like mashed salmon, I was honestly a little disappointed. But after one bite I realized the seasoning was great, and the texture wasn't mushy in the unpleasant canned-food way. The dressed vegetables on the side were also nicely seasoned. The only weak point was the dessert, which was a bit too sweet.

## Koreatown
It's basically just one street with more Korean restaurants than anything else. I went outside mealtime, so I didn't stay long.  
The only thing that felt especially worth checking out was PAT, a larger Korean supermarket that's supposedly cheaper than H Mart. They also sell taiyaki there.  
If you're not specifically craving Korean food or interested in Korean products, I don't think this area is a must.

## Kensington Market

{{<img src="/images/toronto_trip/kensington.jpg" alt="Kensington Market">}}

It's not really a market so much as a neighborhood. The shops can roughly be divided into food and drinks, artsy little stores, and vintage shops.  
There are murals and graffiti all over the place, and the whole area has a really lively vibe.

## Chinatown

It's right next to Kensington Market, so if you keep walking you'll kind of just end up there by accident.  
Even the banks in this area have Chinese translations, and there are all kinds of Chinese food options. Unlike in Montreal, where it sometimes feels like Chinese food means dumplings and then more dumplings.  
For some reason there are also a lot of souvenir shops here, but most of the souvenirs are made in China, which completely killed my desire to buy anything.

## The Planet Traveler Hostel

If I had to sum it up in one sentence: this hostel was basically flawless.  
The common area was big, with people chatting, hanging out, or quietly doing their own thing. The atmosphere was great.  
The shared kitchen had a lot of free stuff like rice, seasonings, coffee, tea bags, and so on. It was also very well equipped, to the point that it even had a rice cooker. Breakfast included a huge amount of bread, jam, cereal, and milk for guests to help themselves to.  
The front door, floor door, and room door all required key cards, so it felt very secure. Each room had its own bathroom, there were personal lockers for luggage, and the beds were clean and comfortable.

The rooftop view was amazing, and every night they hosted free events like movie night, comedy night, and bingo.
