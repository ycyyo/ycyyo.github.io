## Niagara Falls

Niagara is a waterfall. Nicaragua is a country. They are not related in any way.

In winter, you can't take the boat at Niagara Falls. Most day tours replace it with the Journey Behind the Falls, which I heard wasn't especially worth the money, so I decided not to join a tour.

I bought the [GO Transit + WEGO bus Weekend Package](https://www.niagaraparks.com/visit-niagara-parks/plan-your-visit/deals-toronto/) to handle transportation from Toronto Union Station to Niagara Falls. The website also sells a weekday package, but for some reason it's way more expensive than the weekend one.  
GO Transit covers the part from Union Station to Niagara Falls GO, the train and bus station, while WEGO covers the part from Niagara Falls GO to the waterfall area.  
There are only a few direct GO trains to Niagara Falls GO, plus some train-and-bus combinations where you take the train from Union Station to Burlington and then transfer to a bus. Both options take around two and a half hours. If you're taking the bus transfer, you can basically just follow the crowd, so it's not too stressful.

I had checked the morning direct train in advance and got to the station about half an hour early. Only then did I realize GO Transit usually announces the platform just a little before departure. Even on the weekend there weren't many people, though, so there was no need to worry about seats or long lines. GO Transit trains are double-decker, with those four-seat face-to-face arrangements, and that day there were so few people that I got one whole set of four seats to myself.

After arriving at Niagara Falls GO, there was already a WEGO bus waiting outside. You need to scan the barcode from the weekend package when boarding. The bus takes everyone directly to Table Rock. Once you get off, you're right by the falls and the visitor center. Out of all the souvenir shops I saw during the five days in Toronto, the one there was actually one of the more decent ones, second only to the souvenir shop under CN Tower.

Niagara Falls itself was absolutely spectacular. If you walk along the path beside the falls all the way to the City Cruises boat boarding area, it takes around 30 minutes, and that's enough to see the falls from a lot of different angles.  
It was freezing that day, though, so after taking my time to admire the falls, I pretty quickly found a train back.

On the way back, for some reason the next WEGO bus wasn't coming for another 20 minutes. I was worried about missing the GO bus, so I called a Lyft instead. Since it was my first time using the app, I had a 75% off coupon and only paid 2 CAD, which was a great deal.

The GO Transit bus terminal is directly across from the train station, but you need to actually walk into the terminal building before you'll see the buses waiting to depart.

Quick side note on two apps I recommend: the first is Transit, which shows real-time bus and streetcar info and works well in both Montreal and Toronto. The second is Lyft, which is basically like Uber and is sometimes cheaper.

## Distillery District

By the time I got back to downtown Toronto, it was around 4 p.m. and still light out, so I went to walk around the Distillery District near Union Station.

I personally really liked the vibe there. It wasn't crowded, it was quiet, and there were some pretty little shops to browse. It kind of reminded me of Pier-2 in Kaohsiung when there isn't a market going on.
