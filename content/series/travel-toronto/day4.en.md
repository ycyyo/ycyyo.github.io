## University of Toronto

UofT's St. George campus is huge. I started from The Planet Traveler Hostel and walked along College Street.

The first thing I ran into was the UofT bookstore, which was much bigger than both the Universite de Montreal bookstore and Poly's bookstore. It has two floors. The first floor sells clothes, backpacks, and some stationery. The second floor mainly sells books, but also has a smaller selection of clothing plus a medical shop for students in medical-related programs. There were a lot of branded clothing collaborations there too, like Under Armour, Roots, and Lululemon, so it was actually pretty fun to browse.

After passing University College and walking one more block, you'll eventually reach the famous Trinity College. It has a dining hall that looks a lot like the one from the Harry Potter movies, which is why a lot of people treat it as a sightseeing spot. There were quite a few people inside when I visited, though, so I didn't dare walk in or take photos. The tables inside are not long Harry Potter-style tables, but regular six- to eight-person tables arranged in rows. The part that really feels similar is the ceiling and the architectural details.
That day, most of the people on campus still seemed to be actual students rather than tourists, so there was something kind of fun about walking through someone else's college campus.

After walking around St. George campus, I really felt that UofT is a beautiful campus. Even in that awkward in-between season when the snow is half-melted and everything is kind of dirty, you can still take great photos almost anywhere. Definitely worth visiting.

## Royal Ontario Museum (ROM)

Originally, I hadn't planned to visit a museum on this trip. But by day four, I suddenly realized I had already gone to most of the places on my list, and on Tuesdays post-secondary students can enter the ROM for free with a student ID. So after visiting UofT, I made a last-minute decision to stop by.

If you walk along Philosopher's Walk from near Trinity College, the ROM is very close. The museum is divided into several major sections, like dinosaur fossils, the origin of life, endangered animals, minerals, and world cultures. My English clearly wasn't good enough for me to fully appreciate all the exhibit text, though, so I didn't study everything very carefully.

## CN Tower

Like Niagara Falls, CN Tower is one of those places everyone says you have to visit in Toronto. The observation deck gives you a panoramic view of the city, though the ticket costs 40 CAD.

After passing security, you just follow the signs to the elevator. On the way up, you can already see the view outside, and once you arrive, you're at the observation deck. I intentionally booked a ticket close to sunset because I wanted to see both the daytime view and the night view. But if you want to stay for the full transition, it really takes a lot of patience. I spent around two hours up there and still left before it got completely dark.

## St. Lawrence Market

At last, the final day of the trip and the final stop.

St. Lawrence Market feels pretty different from Marche Jean-Talon in Montreal. It also has stalls selling vegetables, fruit, meat, and cheese, but there are also lots of shops that feel more like little grocery stores, selling all kinds of different things. There are also some prepared-food places, dessert shops, and one or two souvenir stores. All I can say is that if Montreal had a place like this, I would absolutely come here all the time.

{{<img src="/images/toronto_trip/lobster.jpg" alt="Lobster Roll of Buster's Sea Cove">}}
▲ Lobster roll from Buster's Sea Cove, 25 CAD, one of the must-eat foods at St. Lawrence Market

The lobster roll and fries were both really good. The only issue was that the portion was a bit too big, so splitting one between two people would probably be perfect.
