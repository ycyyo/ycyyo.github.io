## Telecom Providers

The first thing to take care of after arriving is getting a mobile plan — life is inconvenient without a phone number. Based on seniors' recommendations, I went with Fizz, which is known to be a relatively cheap option.

Fizz offers both eSIM and physical SIM cards. Physical SIMs can be purchased at some metro-station vending machines (see the list linked on Fizz's site); use the number shown there to activate and buy a plan on Fizz's website, which is very convenient.

One advantage of Fizz is flexibility: you can adjust coverage and data anytime and changes apply in the next billing period. Unused data in a month can roll over for up to three months. Over the past six months my travel range included New York (USA), several major Canadian cities and the Banff area — wherever there should be service, Fizz generally had signal and decent speeds.

## Supermarkets

Although North American prices don't feel as extreme as in parts of Europe, eating out plus tax and tip usually costs about 17–20 CAD (~400 NTD) per meal. Buying groceries and cooking at home is much more economical.

Common supermarkets include Maxi, Walmart, Metro and IGA. I go to Maxi most often because it is usually the cheapest; Walmart is my second choice — not as cheap as Maxi but with a wider brand selection. The other two tend to have more prepared foods and sweets but are noticeably pricier.

A side note: in Canada milk is sold in both cartons and plastic bags; the bagged milk is cheaper but you need a plastic pitcher to pour it.

For Asian groceries, T&T seems the most complete — they even carry instant noodles from Taiwanese brands and pineapple cakes — but Montréal's T&T locations are farther from downtown and campus and a bit more expensive. There is also the Korean supermarket chain HMart. I usually go to Marché Fu Tai and Marché Kim Phat; Marché Fu Tai is convenient because it shares a plaza with Walmart, Dollarama and Canadian Tire. Some people say Marché Kim Phat is slightly cheaper than other Asian stores.

If you're looking for farmers' markets, Montréal's most famous are Marché Jean-Talon and Marché Atwater; besides produce they sell maple products and cheeses, and in spring you can find flowers. They tend to close in the afternoon.

## Household Goods

If you don't need anything specialized, head to Dollarama — they sell almost everything except fresh food and it's very cheap. For hardware or small appliances, check out Canadian Tire.

I also recommend visiting thrift stores after you arrive. They can look messy and some items may not be high quality, but you can find useful bargains; if you don't want to take items home when you leave, you can donate them back. Popular thrift stores include Village des Valeurs and Renaissance.

## Transportation

On the island of Montréal, public transit uses the OPUS card system. Unless you travel outside Zone A, a single ride is covered by one ticket regardless of distance; a single fare costs 3.75 CAD. The main transit modes are the metro, buses and the REM. You tap your card to enter platforms or board buses; you don't tap out when leaving. One particularity: you don't need to flag buses — simply stand at the stop and the driver will stop.

After classes start and you've finished course selection, you can apply for a student OPUS card which offers a 4-month pass for 240 CAD. It's a good deal if you ride often, but note the four-month period isn't counted from the activation day by days — for example, if you activate it any day in January, it will still expire at the end of April.

For planning and schedules, besides Google Maps I recommend the Transit app, which is widely used across Canada and can also sell tickets in some regions.

For taxi-like services, Uber and Lyft are both good options; fares vary and one is not consistently cheaper than the other. Lyft often offers promotions for first few rides. For intercity travel, besides trains and buses, consider Poparide (a rideshare app) — it's not a professional taxi service but it uses driver and passenger ratings, so pick drivers with more reviews for safety.

## Takeaway

- Supermarkets: Maxi, Walmart, Metro, IGA
- Asian groceries: Marché Fu Tai, Marché Kim Phat, T&T
- Farmers' markets: Marché Jean-Talon, Marché Atwater
- Household goods: Dollarama, Canadian Tire
- Thrift stores: Village des Valeurs, Renaissance
- Cheap clothing: Winners, Marshalls
- Transit / ride apps: Transit, Uber, Lyft, Poparide
- Food-waste/leftover apps: Too Good To Go
