## About Montreal
Montreal is in Quebec, Canada. It is the second-largest French-speaking city in the world, and people sometimes call it "Little France." Peak tourist season is from summer to fall.

If I had to sum up my impression of the city in one phrase, it would be: cultural melting pot. You see people from all kinds of backgrounds on the street, and it's easy to find food from all over the world.
Canada is officially bilingual, so in many places you can see both English and French on signs. Quebec is a different story, though. You can usually get by in English when talking to people, but a lot of the signs on the street, labels in supermarkets, and even metro announcements are only in French.
Overall, Montreal feels pretty safe. It's fine for girls to go out alone at night. You do see homeless people in metro stations pretty often, but as long as you don't engage and just keep walking, it's okay.
Montreal winters are very cold. Because of the wind, it often feels much colder than the actual temperature. In January and February, a feels-like temperature of around -15 C is extremely common, and on especially cold days it can drop to -30 C. That said, locals told me that winter 2026 was a bit more extreme than usual. In just one month, I experienced rain, snow, and freezing rain, so it was definitely a full package.

## About Polytechnique Montreal
Polytechnique Montreal is affiliated with Universite de Montreal, but it is administratively independent and has its own president and administrative units. One of Poly's characteristics is that the workload is heavier than at many other universities in Quebec, and its engineering ranking is also pretty solid.

Most classes here are taught in French, and only a very small number are offered in English. There are two exchange options: the regular student exchange program, and International Thematic Clusters in Engineering, usually shortened to ITC. Besides exchange, Poly also offers [research internships](https://www.polymtl.ca/futur/en/es/research/internships) for international students.

The program that NYCU has with Poly is ITC. ITC is divided into several fields, but in the winter term only the software track is open for exchange students, and everyone has to pick one field. There are usually five or six technical courses to choose from. On top of that, you can replace one or two courses with a lab internship. Poly also has an agreement with Universite de Montreal that allows Poly exchange students to take French classes there. Out of all these options, you need to take at least four courses.

Everything above was the objective part. Now for my personal opinion.
If you're reading this because you're wondering whether you should go to Poly in the winter term, then I oppose it with both hands and feet. The main reason is the administrative mess between ITC admission and course registration. I'll explain, or rather complain about, that in the next post.
