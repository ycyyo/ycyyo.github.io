Date: 2026/02/24

The main purpose of this post is basically to complain, and to dump all my frustration here so I can write more useful and informative posts later on. (That said, this post will still include some helpful info from the nomination stage up to the start of the semester.)

If you’re thinking about going to Polytechnique Montréal for the winter term, please read this post carefully. Make sure you can stay completely calm through all of the things I’m about to mention before you hit that submit button on your exchange application.

[This page](https://www.polymtl.ca/gopoly/en/new-student-journey/new-student-journey-clusters) summarizes some of the things you need to do before and after the semester starts.

## Incident 1: Admission
On September 12, I received an email from Poly asking me to fill out some online forms before October 1. The email itself was written in French, and the attached fact sheet was also for the French-language exchange program. On top of that, the form had a field asking me to choose my program, but International Thematic Clusters was not even in the list.

I immediately contacted my school's Office of International Affairs to confirm whether I had been nominated for the English-taught ITC program or the French-taught regular Student Exchange program. They told me Poly had sent the wrong nomination form and that they needed to confirm with the partner school again.

By September 21 I still hadn't heard anything, so I asked again. This time the international office sent me a new link. It turned out to be exactly the same as the original Poly form, except the language had changed from French to English. The winter-term ITC option was still missing from the dropdown. So again, they had to confirm things with the partner school. In the end I was finally able to submit the form, and on October 28 I received my offer from Poly. The email and the attached PDF were still entirely in French.

The most annoying part of the admission process was having to submit a birth certificate in either English or French. In Taiwan, birth certificates are originally issued by hospitals, and the electronic record is kept by the household registration office. You can get a copy from household registration offices anywhere in Taiwan, but only in Chinese. That means you need a translation and court notarization on top of that. I handled it by finding a translation agency that also offered notarization services, and the whole process took around two to three business days.

## Incident 2: French Class
In the previous post, I mentioned that Poly has an arrangement with UdeM that allows exchange students to take French classes there. Since it's cross-registration between two schools, you have to use different platforms, and you also need to take a placement test before course registration.

The registration website, AEHE BCI, seemed completely inaccessible from Taiwan. I tried the NYCU dorm network, Chunghwa Telecom mobile hotspot, and my home internet, which I think was also Chunghwa. Every single attempt ended in a connection timeout. I emailed the relevant office at UdeM once, and the message got forwarded to the office at Poly, but after that I never got any reply. So I gave up on taking French.

In the first week of school, we finally met the people in charge of the exchange program, which I'll call SEP from here on. I told one of them about this issue, and she said we should have emailed them earlier so they could help. The problem was that we didn't even know how to contact SEP at the time.
Later we learned an important lesson: **for basically anything, email `etudiant.echange[at]polymtl.ca` first**. They will actually try to coordinate with other departments for you. If you contact other offices directly, there's a good chance you'll just get ignored.

## Incident 3: Course Registration, Part 1
When you fill out the online admission form, you also need to list the courses you want to take. This turns out to have absolutely nothing to do with actual course registration.
Poly's academic calendar shows the course registration periods under Course Selection or Modification. There are two stages, and the second one is more like the add/drop period after classes begin. For Winter 2026, the two stages were December 10-22, 2025 and January 5-21, 2026.

Because I had listed a French course in the admission form, but later realized I wouldn't be able to take it, I logged into the system on December 10 to modify my courses. The moment I opened it, I discovered that my schedule was completely blank. They do not pre-enroll you based on what you submitted earlier. I quietly complained to myself about what the earlier form was even for, but course registration itself shouldn't be that hard, right? So I started figuring it out.

There are very few ITC courses available, only five in total. My plan was to register for all of them and then drop some later if needed before midterms. That's when I ran into the first issue: how exactly is this registration system supposed to work?

At the time I still had a few other questions, so I emailed the admission office. My message asked three questions, but their reply answered only one of them. So I had to spend a while basically doing divination on my own before figuring this out.

## Incident 4: Course Registration, Part 2
Once I finally understood how the system worked, the next problem showed up immediately: out of the five available courses, two couldn't be selected because they were already full. Poly requires students to take at least 12 credits, which means four courses, in order to count as a full-time student. You need full-time status to get a student ID, and you need a student ID to apply for the student transit pass.

So we emailed the admission inbox again. The replies we got, twice, were honestly kind of incredible. They were completely unrelated to the actual question.
At that point it was obvious this channel was not going to produce any useful information, so I asked my school's Office of International Affairs for help. They emailed SEP for us, but by then Poly's administrative offices were already on Christmas break, so we didn't receive any reply at all until the semester started.

When the second round of course registration opened, I went back into the system and tried registering for those two courses again. This time it finally worked. But then I immediately discovered another problem: three of the courses had schedule conflicts, as shown below.

In other words, among those five courses, there was no possible combination that would let me reach the required 12 credits.

This was the point where things finally started moving in a better direction. Before the semester started, Poly held an orientation for international students, though it was in French. Out of pure desperation, another NYCU exchange student and I went anyway. That was also when we first met SEP in person. Once we explained the scheduling problem, they immediately said they would try to fix it. The next day, one of the conflicting lecture sessions was removed, and although two of the lab sections still seemed to overlap, the labs only met every other week, marked B1 and B2 in the schedule, so they actually alternated and did not conflict.

That was also when SEP told us that there were only two ITC exchange students in the winter term, the two of us, so the English welcome meeting had been combined with the one for international research interns. Of course, it was scheduled at a time when we already had class.

## Side Story: Course Syllabi
Before going abroad, the CS department office at NYCU required us to submit a tentative course list and the full syllabus for each course, so they could determine whether the credits could count as free electives or professional electives.
Poly's website had almost none of that information. It only showed very short course descriptions plus the class times and locations. I sent countless emails to different offices at Poly asking for detailed syllabi, and it was completely useless.
In the end, it turned out the department office at home also accepted the documents after departure. Realistically, they wouldn't know exactly when you left anyway. During the first class meeting, each professor handed out the syllabus, so I just asked classmates in Taiwan to help submit them for me. The important thing is that all the required signatures on the tentative course list still have to be done before you leave.

## Incident 5: A Course Was Suddenly Cancelled
Around the first week of the add/drop period, I suddenly got a notice saying one of the courses had been cancelled and would not be offered that semester.
Without that course, reaching 12 credits would become impossible, so we immediately contacted SEP. The next day we were told the course had been reopened. In reality, though, there were only five students in the class.

## Incident 6: Directed Reading in Computer and Software
Directed Reading in Computer and Software was one of the remaining four courses. At Poly, classroom codes usually look like L, M, A, B, or C followed by three or four digits. But for this course, the schedule listed the location simply as DEPART. Starting from the first week of the semester, we emailed the professor in charge twice to ask where the class was actually held. Even two weeks later, with the add/drop deadline approaching, we still had no reply.

With no other option, we went back to SEP. They asked us to email the professor one more time and cc the SEP inbox. Two days later, on the very last day of add/drop, the professor finally replied. The email explained that for this course, students had to find their own supervising professor, who would assign reading materials and require a final report at the end of the semester.

The other NYCU exchange student found one of his course instructors and solved the issue pretty quickly. I wasn't especially interested in the research areas of the professors teaching my courses, so I emailed other professors instead. Over two weeks, I sent emails to four professors one by one. Only the fourth one agreed to supervise me. The others did not even reply to say no. That was how this whole mess finally ended, about a month into the semester.

Honestly, before emailing that fourth professor, I had already started thinking that if this one also ignored me, I was going to write a formal complaint letter to both Poly and the NYCU international office, and write in my midterm reflection that I'd never recommend anyone go to Poly on exchange.
For ITC course registration, there is basically no real choice. If you don't do a lab internship, then you are stuck with these fixed four courses. In that situation, I genuinely don't understand why I had to spend this much effort just to make my own schedule work. Judging from how hard it was to find a professor for Directed Reading, I doubt finding a lab internship would have been easy either. I know the winter-term ITC option has only existed for a few years, but if the support system is still this underdeveloped, then I really don't think Poly should have opened winter-term exchange at all.

Anyway, that's pretty much the full list of nonsense I ran into at Poly. Thanks for reading all the way to the end.
